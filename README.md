# 📊 NovaRetail+ | Customer Behavior Analysis 

## 📌 Context and Business Challenge
NovaRetail+ seeks to better understand its customers’ behavior and the factors that may be related to the annual revenue generated by each customer. From a business perspective, understanding these relationships allows for the identification of behavioral patterns that may be relevant to areas such as marketing, customer retention, and revenue growth. This project uses exploratory analysis and correlation analysis techniques to study the relationship between different customer behavior variables and their annual revenue. Important: The analysis identifies associations between variables. These associations do not necessarily imply a causal relationship.
--- 

## 🎯 Objective of the Analysis
The main objective is to identify which customer behavior factors are most strongly associated with annual revenue.

### Analysis Questions
The project seeks to answer the following questions:
- Which variables are most strongly related to annual revenue?
- Is there a relationship between purchase frequency and annual income?
- How do monthly visits relate to income?
- What is the relationship between advertising spend and revenue generated?
- What association exists between Premium membership and annual income?
- What is the relationship between churn and income?
- What behavioral patterns may be relevant to business decisions?

## 📊 Data Source
The analysis uses a customer behavior dataset:
- novaretail_customer_behavior_2024.csv The dataset contains information related to various customer characteristics and behaviors during the analyzed period. The information is used to study the relationships between behavioral variables and customers’ annual income.

## 🔎 Methodology
The analysis was conducted through a process of exploration, data preparation, visualization, and statistical analysis.

### 1. Loading and Initial Exploration
An initial review of the dataset was conducted to understand:
- Data structure.
- Available variables.
- Data types.
- General characteristics of the data.

### 2. Data Preparation and Validation
Data quality was reviewed prior to analysis, including validation of the variables used and their preparation for the various statistical techniques.

### 3. Exploratory Analysis
Descriptive statistics and visualizations were used to understand the distribution and behavior of the main variables.

### 4. Correlation Analysis
Different association techniques were applied depending on the type of variables analyzed:
- Pearson: to analyze linear relationships between numerical variables.
- Spearman: to evaluate monotonic relationships.
- Point-biserial: to analyze the association between a binary variable and a numerical variable.
- Cramér’s V: to analyze the association between categorical variables.

### 5. Interpretation of Results
The results of the different techniques were compared and interpreted from a business perspective, seeking to identify which variables show relevant associations with annual income.

## 📈 Key Findings
The analysis identified varying levels of association between behavioral variables and customers’ annual income.
- Monthly customer purchases showed the strongest positive relationship with annual income.
- Monthly visits showed a moderate positive association.
- Advertising spend showed a weak positive relationship.
- Premium membership and customer churn showed relatively weak associations with annual income.

## Future Improvements
- Build predictive models for annual income.
- Incorporate additional customer segmentation.
- Explore causal relationships beyond correlation analysis.

### 🛒 Monthly Purchases
The number of purchases made per month showed the strongest positive relationship with annual revenue among the variables analyzed. This result indicates that purchase frequency is a relevant variable for understanding the behavior associated with revenue generation.

### 👀 Monthly Visits
Monthly visits showed a moderate positive association with annual revenue. This reveals a relationship between the level of customer interaction with the platform and recorded revenue.

### 📢 Advertising Spend
Advertising spend showed a weak positive relationship with annual revenue. This result should be interpreted as an association observed in the data and not as evidence that higher advertising spend directly causes higher revenue.

### ⭐ Premium Membership
Premium membership showed a relatively weak association with annual revenue in this analysis.

### 🔄 Churn
The churn variable also showed a relatively weak association with annual revenue.

## 💡 Business Interpretation
The results show that variables related to purchasing activity and customer interaction are relevant for understanding the differences observed in annual income. In particular, monthly purchase frequency stands out among the analyzed variables as it exhibits the strongest positive association with annual income. The other variables should be interpreted in light of the level of association found and the scope of the analysis.

## 🚀 Business Recommendations
Based on the results obtained, the following courses of action can be proposed to deepen the analysis and support decision-making:

1.    Analyze purchase frequency: Consider purchase frequency as a relevant variable for segmenting customers and studying different revenue-generation patterns.

2.    Delve deeper into customer behavior: Analyze purchase frequency, visits, and other behavioral variables together to identify customer profiles with distinct patterns.

3.    Evaluate marketing strategies: Use the association analysis between advertising spend and revenue as a starting point for more detailed studies on the performance of marketing investments.

4.    Analyze Premium membership: Delve deeper into the behavior of Premium customers and compare it with other segments to better understand its relationship to revenue.

5.    Delve deeper into churn: Analyze the behavior of customers who cancel their service and examine other variables that may help explain customer retention or churn.

6.    Move Toward Predictive Models As the next step, the analysis can be expanded using predictive models to study which variables best explain or predict annual revenue. These recommendations represent lines of analysis derived from the observed results. They do not imply causal relationships that have not been demonstrated in this project.

## ⚠️ Limitations of the Analysis
This project is based primarily on exploratory analysis and measures of association. Therefore:
- A correlation does not prove causation.
- A weak association does not necessarily mean that a variable is unimportant to the business.
- The results depend on the variables available in the dataset.
- To establish causal relationships, additional analysis would be necessary.
- Predictive models require an additional stage of modeling and validation.
